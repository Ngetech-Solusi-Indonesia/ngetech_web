# Product

## Platform

web

## Users
Three audiences with equal weight, all in or near Bandung:
- Business owners and small-to-medium businesses (UMKM) whose operations still run on paper or spreadsheets (stock, attendance, records).
- Schools and institutions (principals, admin offices, foundations) that need attendance and internal systems.
- Event organisers, wedding vendors, and brand activations that need a photobooth.

Their job on this site: understand what Ngetech can build for them and start a conversation on WhatsApp.

## Product Purpose
Ngetech Solusi Indonesia is a five-person software team in Bandung that builds custom software, including software that runs with physical devices. Success is a visitor opening a WhatsApp chat about a project.

## Positioning
Confirmed differentiators (user-confirmed 2026-10-07):
- One team handles both software and hardware (RFID readers, cameras, printers).
- Local to Bandung; the team can come on site to install and train.
- Built to the client's own workflow, not an off-the-shelf subscription app.
- Scale and cost that make sense for UMKM.

## Capabilities and Constraints
Products and their true status:
- **Inventory system:** in use by a real client (client not named).
- **NgeBooth** (photobooth app): internal testing.
- **RFID attendance:** in development, not deployed anywhere.

Constraints: Indonesian is the primary language, English secondary. Contact is via WhatsApp and email only (no form). Domain: ngetech.studio.

Open (not yet provided): WhatsApp number, team names and roles, legal entity name, contact email, GitHub org.

## Brand Commitments
- Name: Ngetech Solusi Indonesia ("Ngetech").
- NgeBooth has its own identity (lime accent, Poppins wordmark), which is kept inside NgeBooth's own surfaces.
- Voice: plain, concrete Indonesian. No marketing buzzwords, no forced puns on the name.
- Visual direction (chosen 2026-10-07): the category standard for a software company, executed straight. Craft bar: Linear and Vercel.

## Product Principles
1. Every claim on the page is true today; status labels never overstate.
2. Show the work doing its job rather than describing it.
3. Talk to non-technical owners in their own terms: their problem first, technology second.
4. The first contact should feel like messaging a local team, not filling in a vendor's form.
